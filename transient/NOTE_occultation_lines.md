# Note: occultation scenario, delays, and line profiles

## Continuum (narrow bump + UV downturn)

- A slow TDE at r = 2 light days heats debris that shear stretches into
  two raised spiral arms on the disk, viewed at i = 60 deg.
- The heated arm emits hydrogen Balmer continuum: truncated at the
  3646 A edge, so the bump is narrower than any blackbody.
- The same raised arm blocks our view of the hot inner disk, which
  suppresses the UV (the 2019 spectrum falls below 2000 in the far UV).
- Both the bump and the UV downturn come from one structure; the
  downturn only appears at high inclination.

## Delays

- During the flare, ZTF shows the red bands lagging the blue: r lags g
  by ~3 days and i by ~7.5 days, longer than a standard quiescent disk
  predicts.
- In this scenario the flare adds a bright reprocessing structure at
  2 light days, so variable light in the flare state is weighted to
  larger radii and the delays lengthen; quantitative matching is still
  open.

## Line profiles

- Hbeta and Hgamma brighten during the flare; the responding component
  is narrow (~1700 km/s) and centered; Mg II responds later (2021).
- Modeled with the same geometry: the quiescent line is a Keplerian
  ring at the arm radius, the flare adds the heated-arm material.
- This reproduces the quiescent profiles and, naturally, the observed
  red-favored response asymmetry (prograde rotation).
- Open: the observed response peaks at zero velocity, and its width
  implies a much smaller black-hole mass than expected if the line gas
  really orbits at 2 light days; the response may instead be the
  ordinary broad-line region echoing the flare.
